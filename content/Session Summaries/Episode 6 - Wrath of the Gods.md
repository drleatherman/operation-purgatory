## Short Summary
> The party traveled west across Purgatory's dusty plains in their Warthog, encountering an overturned vehicle along the way. A dangerous, worm-like creature burst from the wreck and attacked, burning Mike Jackson with its acidic tongue before being shot apart — though the acid splatter wounded the shooter as well. The wreck yielded useful supplies, including oxygen tanks and a patch kit, along with a mysterious red masquerade mask. More troubling were the large, clawed bipedal footprints heading west — the same direction as the party. Following the tracks up a mountain trail, they discovered a massive blast door built into the rock, stuck partially open. Peering inside revealed rust, broken terminals, and drag marks suggesting something had been hauling bodies. They rigged claymore mines at the entrance and used a still-active terminal to draw out whatever lurked within. A voice warned them they were in a quarantine zone infected by a virus called "the body politik," which caused the body to violently revolt against the mind. When heavy movement sounded from deep inside, the party fled. The blast door opened, the claymores detonated, and two horrifying mutated creatures emerged — the second a twelve-foot behemoth wielding a railgun-like weapon. In the ensuing chaos, a rear tire was blown out and Reginald was launched from the vehicle and cut down by gunfire before a blinding flash halted the pursuit. The party escaped into the dark, changed the tire, and pressed onward.
> Continuing west, they followed James's crude map toward a towering rock spire with deep grooves along its sides, which James hoped might restore the memories stolen from him by the Abattoir company. Crossing into the planet's sun-scorched light side, they ascended the spire's winding path and forced open a blast door halfway up, releasing a cool haze. Inside, they found Goddard — the expedition's former cook, now erratic and devoted, with a hand where his foot should have been. He guided them through blood-soaked living quarters, past two figures mindlessly trying to force themselves through a wall grate, and into a prayer room dominated by a grotesque mound of flesh sculpted into the shape of an angel, with a ruby set in one eye socket. The sight shook the party deeply, though James felt a sudden surge of adrenaline. Goddard spoke of the Last Prophet — an ancient, all-knowing being who communicated only through the facility's systems and had recently delivered one final message about a gate of envy being opened before going silent. As James stood in that strange, blood-soaked place, fragments of memory began to surface: the mission had never truly been about recovering Aidai artifacts. That was the cover story. Abattoir had sent them to find a gateway somewhere on Purgatory — and whatever they had discovered had been enough to wipe their memories clean. Now, deep inside the spire with no clear plan, the party faced the growing certainty that they were entangled in something far larger and more dangerous than they yet understood.

## Full Summaryr
The party set out across the dusty, wind-swept plains of Purgatory in their Warthog, driving toward the distant wall of light that marked the planet's sun-baked side. The sky above them churned with rolling clouds, and the open sides of the vehicle offered little protection from the biting gusts of dust that stung their eyes and skin. As they pushed westward, a shape emerged from the haze — an overturned Warthog, half-buried under a layer of dust, crawling with small lizards that slipped in and out of its compartments. It had clearly been there for a long time.

When one of the compartments was thrown open, a winged, worm-like creature burst out — a fleshy, scale-ringed thing with a circular mouth lined with serrated teeth. Ennis drove his fist into it with tremendous force, sending it skidding back several feet, while Mike Jackson brought his boot down hard enough to flatten part of its lower body. The creature refused to die, however, and lashed out with a ropey, acidic tongue that coiled around Mike's ankle and began burning through his suit and into his skin. A revolver was drawn and the creature was blasted to pieces, but the spray of acidic fluid caught the shooter as well, leaving burning splotches across exposed skin that worsened in the cool breeze. The party patched what they could and searched the wreck, turning up a vivid red masquerade mask adorned with feathers and rhinestones, two full oxygen tanks tucked beneath a blanket in the back, and a patch kit.

Before they left, they noticed something else — large, bipedal footprints pressed deep into the dust, clawed at the toes, and heading west. The same direction they were going. The party climbed back into the Warthog and followed the tracks through the sprawling desert washes, passing strange cacti and swollen-leaved plants that seemed perpetually bloated. After several miles, the prints veered away from the wash and climbed toward a saddle between two neighboring mountains. James's map — scratched onto a piece of rubber with a bit of skin still clinging to it — told them to keep going straight, but curiosity and caution won out, and they turned to follow the tracks up the mountain trail.

After about twenty minutes of driving, they crested the saddle and found a massive blast door, fifteen feet tall and thirty feet wide, built into the rock face. It had tried to close and failed, left stuck open just enough to fit a fist through. Alongside it sat a dim computer terminal, still faintly powered. Peering through the crack with a scoped weapon, the party could make out red rust spiderwebs draped across broken terminals inside, a hazy atmosphere, and a second door about twenty-five feet in — also cracked open. They also noticed, with a creeping unease, that the footprints didn't stop at the door. They went through it. And alongside them, beginning somewhere up the mountain trail, were drag marks — body-sized.

The party devised a plan. Two claymore mines were rigged at the crack of the door — one at the top, one at the bottom — with a pull-cord tripwire stretched across the opening. Mike moved to the terminal to make noise and draw out whatever was inside, and the terminal crackled to life with a voice that was not entirely welcoming. It warned them they were not welcome, that they were in a quarantine zone, and that a virus — the body politik — was spreading through the facility. The voice described it in chilling terms: the virus caused the body to turn against the mind, body parts defecting violently or simply tearing away, leaving the host without executive function. The voice claimed to have preserved itself through technology for a long time. Then came the sound of heavy metal sliding from somewhere deep within, and the party bolted for the Warthog.

The blast door ground open, and the claymores detonated. One mutated figure — a humanoid with two hands growing upward from its neck and a bull nose piercing — was obliterated instantly, its torso tumbling out onto the ground. Behind it came something far worse: a twelve-foot-tall creature with taut skin stretched over rippling muscles, three arms, and a face — if it could be called that — covered in a grotesque scattering of extra ears, noses, and mouths. It raised a massive railgun-like weapon and opened fire. Mike blasted it with his pulse rifle, landing a solid hit, but the creature barely flinched. Ennis threw the Warthog into a wild series of evasive maneuvers, but the creature's return fire found its mark, blowing out a rear tire and sending the vehicle dragging and lurching across the ground. In the chaos, a hard bump launched Reginald into the air and directly into the line of fire, where he was cut down by a spray of bullets and tumbled off the back of the vehicle. A blinding flash of light erupted near the creature — and then nothing gave chase. The party escaped into the dark.

They pulled over long enough for Ennis to jack up the Warthog and swap in the spare tire, working with the practiced ease of someone who genuinely enjoyed this sort of thing, while the others kept watch. With the vehicle running smoothly again, they descended back to the wash and continued west. After about an hour, the landscape changed dramatically. The rock ahead looked as though impossibly large hands had reached down and pulled it upward, stretching it into a towering spire with deep groove-like markings running along its sides. A path wound upward around its exterior, and James — staring at it with wide, uncertain eyes — muttered that he hoped seeing it would shake loose whatever the Abattoir company had taken from him.

As they crossed into the light side of the planet, the storm clouds ended like a curtain being drawn back, and beyond them lay the burning fields — a vast, blinding, utterly lifeless expanse baked under a sun so fierce it was nearly impossible to look at. No plants, no movement, nothing. The temperature inside their vac suits climbed sharply, and even with the suits' protection, the heat pressed in around them. It was beautiful in its desolation, and deeply unsettling for the same reason. They made their way up the path encircling the spire and found a blast door halfway up, overlooking the burning fields, its terminal broken and dark. A crowbar from the Warthog made short work of the door.

As the door was forced open, a thick haze poured out into the hot air. Inside, a man looked up from where he had been resting and immediately demanded they close the door — he needed his beauty rest, and they were letting the cool air out. He wore a wife-beater and a gold chain, and where one of his feet should have been, there was instead a hand. He was irritable and erratic, but he welcomed them to what he called reception — a place of worship, he said, where they had found God. The dim track lights along the floors and ceilings barely illuminated the room, and the party switched on their flashlights to see properly. Security cameras watched from the corners.

The man — Goddard, as James would eventually recognize him — led them through the facility. The living quarters were a ruin of overturned bunks and blood-slicked floors, with blood-streaked handprints smeared across every wall. In the far corner, two figures were mindlessly trying to jam themselves through a wall grate, attempting it head-first, feet-first, and every other way, over and over. The air tasted of iron-rich blood, and Goddard remarked that he found it rather invigorating. He warned them away from the nightstands, where something had been left and gone very bad, and then led them onward to the prayer room.

In the center of the prayer room sat a mound of flesh. It had been sculpted — bandaged and shaped — into the rough form of an angel, with a one-inch ruby set into one of its eye sockets. It was utterly horrifying, and the sight of it hit the party like a physical blow. One character was left shaken and spiraling, their nerves frayed. James, by contrast, felt a sudden electric rush of adrenaline surge through him. Goddard spoke of the Last Prophet — an ancient, all-knowing being who had been there at the beginning of Purgatory and who communicated only through the facility's computer systems and cameras, never showing himself. The Prophet had recently delivered one final message: the gate of envy had been thrust open, and because of it, darkness was sweeping the land. Then he had gone silent and refused all contact.

It was James who finally placed Goddard — the expedition's former cook, brought along because the team had planned to stay for a while. He had not been this devoted before. He had not been missing a foot before. James also found fragments of memory surfacing as he stood in that strange, blood-soaked place: the mission had not truly been about recovering artifacts from the Aidai. That had been the cover story. The Abattoir company had sent them here because they suspected a gateway existed somewhere on Purgatory — and that was what they had come to find. Whatever they had discovered, it had been enough to wipe their memories clean. Now, standing inside the spire with a mutated cook for a guide and a flesh-angel watching from the center of the room, the party had no clear plan — only the certainty that they were already deep inside something they did not yet fully understand.

## NPCs
### Reginald
A companion whose memory was wiped by the Abattoir family company after a previous mission. He was killed during the escape from the mountain pass blast door when he was launched into the line of fire.

### James
A survivor of a previous mission with Reginald who possesses spotty memories and provided a rough map to the spire. He hopes reaching the spire will help him recover memories stolen by the Abattoir company. He recognized Goddard as the expedition's former cook.

### Father Fresco
A figure mentioned by the party who informed them about the corporate practice of wiping memories to safeguard discoveries, and who apparently had a falling out with the worshipers at the spire.

### The Terminal Voice
A cryptic entity speaking through the mountain facility's intercom system. It warned the party of a quarantine and described the 'body politik' virus, claiming to have preserved itself through technology for a long time.

### Neck-Hand Mutant
A horrific humanoid figure with two hands growing out of its neck and a bull nose piercing. It was killed instantly when the party's claymore trap detonated as the blast door opened.

### Three-Armed Monstrosity
A twelve-foot-tall creature with taut skin stretched over rippling muscles, three arms, and multiple ears, noses, and mouths scattered across its body. It wielded a large railgun-like weapon and blew out the Warthog's tire before the party escaped.

### Goddard
The former cook for the expedition who has become a devoted worshiper within the spire. He is physically deformed — missing a foot which has been replaced by a hand-like growth — and exhibits an irritable, erratic personality. He gave the party a tour of the facility and spoke of the Last Prophet.

### The Last Prophet
An ancient, all-knowing entity who resides within the spire facility. He has sequestered himself from his followers and communicates only through the base's computer systems and cameras. He reportedly warned that the 'gate of envy' had been thrust open, bringing darkness to the land.

### Gabriel
Not a living NPC but a grotesque 'angel' sculpted from a mound of bandaged flesh and limbs, serving as the central object of worship in the spire's prayer room, with a one-inch ruby set into one eye socket.

## Locations
### Purgatory
A planet characterized by perpetually dust-filled skies and a distinct light side where the clouds break into blinding brightness, with a tidal zone and a barren burning fields region.

### Overturned Warthog
A derelict vehicle found in the desert washes, covered in dust and serving as a nest for small lizards and a winged, acidic worm-creature that ambushed the party.

### Purgatory Desert Washes
Wide, sprawling desert washes flanked by cliff faces and strange flora — cacti and plants with perpetually swollen leaves. The party followed large bipedal tracks through this terrain.

### Mountain Saddle Blast Door
A massive sliding blast door, about fifteen feet tall and thirty feet wide, located in a mountain saddle. It was found stuck slightly ajar with a dim computer terminal nearby and drag marks leading into it.

### The Spire
A massive rock formation that appears to have been pulled and stretched upward, featuring deep groove-like markings and a path encircling its exterior. It overlooks the burning fields, and a blast door halfway up leads to a carved facility within.

### The Burning Fields
A blindingly bright, completely barren wasteland on the light side of Purgatory where temperatures reach around 130 degrees Fahrenheit and no plant life exists.

### The Spire Facility
A complex carved directly into the rock of the spire, featuring a lounge reception area, disheveled living quarters, and a prayer room. All rooms are permeated by a bloody, smoky haze and decorated with blood-streaked handprints.

### The Prayer Room
A chamber within the spire containing a grotesque monument to an angel named Gabriel, constructed from sculpted and bandaged flesh and limbs, with a one-inch ruby set into an eye socket.
