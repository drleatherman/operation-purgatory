## Summary
The tension in **Ennis**'s seventh-floor apartment was already thick when **James**, his raw and exposed skin still glistening from the removal of his rubber-like outer shell, pressed a makeshift map into the group's hands. He had etched the route onto a piece of rubber marked with his torn skin, tracing a path due west from *Cudassam* to an ancient spire overlooking The Burning Fields — the site of a previous corporate expedition that neither he nor Reginald could fully remember. Before the group could fully process what they were looking at, the building's loudspeaker crackled to life with a cold, corporate voice announcing a lockdown, citing a breach of company policy. The door to the hallway clicked shut and locked from the outside, sealing them in.

With security teams sweeping floor by floor, the party quickly settled on a plan: descend through the ventilation shafts to the storage room several floors below, where they could slip out undetected. Mike Jackson went first, tying a rope to a wall rail and lowering himself feet-first into the tight, two-foot-wide shaft. Partway down, his hands and feet slipped against the slick walls, and he grabbed the rope in desperation, suffering severe rope burn that tore the skin from his palms and exposed the tendons beneath. He made it to the bottom, bloodied and hurting, but alive enough to peer through the vent grate into the storage room below. Seeing only stacked crates and no sign of guards, he gave a thumbs-up to those waiting above.

What followed was a grim parade of descents. **Tabatha** came next, only to find the rope now slick with Mike's blood, and she slipped and crashed into the storage room below. Reginald, freshly flayed and raw-skinned, had to be talked into the shaft with promises of safety — and possibly an orgy — before he plummeted straight down the blood-slicked rope and landed squarely on his head, leaving a long vertical blood streak smeared down the wall from the vent to the floor. James, hardier than anyone gave him credit for, made it down cleanly and immediately began rummaging through the storage crates for something to cover his exposed skin, eventually pulling out a bizarre collection of Elizabethan-style theatrical costumes. Meanwhile, Ennis frantically swept the apartment for incriminating rubber shavings left behind from James's shed shell, stuffed them into a plastic bag, and threw it down the vent just as a heavy knock sounded at his door.

Two corporate security agents in dark suits and sunglasses — both named Bob — forced their way into Ennis's quarters, citing bylaws that required full cooperation during inspections. They donned surgical gloves with unsettling calm, and while one watched Ennis with unblinking attention, the other tore the room apart: flipping the mattress, rifling through drawers, pulling sheets from the bed. Ennis met their questions with dry composure, even asking whether all the mess was strictly necessary, and the Bobs assured him it was. Finding nothing, they handed him a blank business card reading only "The Bobs," reminded him to report anything untoward, and locked the door behind them on their way out. Ennis exhaled, then climbed into the vent himself and made it down to the storage room below.

The group regrouped in the dusty storage room, tending to wounds with strips of cloth torn from the costume crates. Mike's hands were wrapped so thickly they resembled cartoon gloves, and Tabatha had used a stimpack to steady herself after her fall. James, now dressed in a ruffled Elizabethan ensemble, described in quiet, haunted terms what it had meant to be a corporate lab rat — the experiments, the blades, the screaming friends — while Tabatha found a denim jacket with a skull on the back that fit her as though it had been made for her. With the lockdown still active, Mike studied the storage room's keypad, blew a handful of dust across the buttons to reveal which four were most worn, and cracked the code with startling precision. The door swung open onto a quiet, empty hallway.

The party moved through the concrete stairwell that hugged the building's central pillar, descending to the third floor just as the intercom announced the lockdown had been lifted. Employees streamed out of their rooms and filled the corridors, providing perfect cover. Tabatha slipped into a nearby restroom and discovered an abandoned backpack tucked in a stall corner. Inside were two directional explosive devices, each stamped with the words "front toward enemy." Mike identified them as claymore mines — inert for now, but capable of devastating anyone unlucky enough to be on the wrong end of them — and the party quietly tucked them away as a tactical asset.

Getting out of the building meant getting a vehicle, and getting a vehicle meant navigating the station's notoriously slow approval process — or knowing the right person. Ennis knew Gunner, an old friend who managed the warehouse office and held the keys to the Warthogs parked on the first floor. After a bit of friendly negotiation, Ennis handed over his prized, well-thumbed collection of pornographic magazines as a bribe, leaving his dignity on the desk in exchange for a set of keys and a two-hour window under the pretense of a test drive. Gunner's only condition was that Ennis keep an eye out for a missing driver named Lenny, who had driven off into the wasteland and never come back.

The party loaded into the eight-person military transport, with **James** and **Reginald** hidden on the floor beneath blankets while Mike took the wheel and Ennis rode shotgun. At the airlock bay, a security officer with a data tab logged their names and their cover story — a routine test drive of a freshly repaired vehicle — and handed them a radio before waving them through. The heavy blast doors sealed behind them, and the chamber filled with the hiss of depressurization. Then the outer doors swung open, and the dusky, alien twilight of Purgatory stretched out before them — unbreathable, vast, and waiting — as the Warthog rolled forward into the world beyond the station's walls, pointed west toward the ancient spire on James's map.

## NPCs
### Father Fresco
Leader of the Church of the Last Scion, mentioned in the recap as having knowledge of the ancient Adai civilization and their purification ritual involving seven gates. He has participated in forays into ancient structures and explained that the corporation wipes the memories of those who make significant discoveries.

### James
A survivor of a previous corporate expedition whose memory was partially wiped. His rubber-like outer shell was recently removed, leaving his skin raw and exposed. He etches a map to the ancient spire on a piece of his shed skin and joins the party's escape, hiding in the Warthog under a blanket.

### Reginald
A companion suffering from a leprosy-like condition who had been hiding in Ennis's apartment. He is reluctant to return to the lower levels of the facility and suffers a hard fall during the vent descent, landing on his head and leaving a blood streak on the wall.

Two corporate security agents in suits and sunglasses, both named Bob. They conduct an aggressive search and seizure of Ennis's quarters, citing corporate bylaws, donning surgical gloves, and ransacking the room before leaving Ennis a blank business card identifying them only as 'The Bobs.'

An old friend of Ennis described as more of a fighting type, who manages the warehouse office and controls access to vehicle keys. A driver known for telling terrible jokes who reportedly drove off into the wasteland and never returned. Gunner asks Ennis to look for him as a condition of borrowing the Warthog.

A station employee stationed at the airlock who checks identities and logs vehicle departures using a data tab. He clears the party for their two-hour test drive and provides them with a radio.
