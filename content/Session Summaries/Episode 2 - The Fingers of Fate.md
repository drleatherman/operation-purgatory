## Summary
The scouting party was making their way across the dusty, twilight-drenched surface of Purgatory — a world locked in perpetual dusk — when something massive and wrong came barreling out of the dim horizon. The creature moved like a big cat but was wrong in all the ways that mattered: three lion-like paws and one leathery, bird-like talon, its fur patchy and mangy, its flesh discolored with sickness. It hit the group like a freight train, snatching Alabama Redinger clean out of the vehicle with its claws and hauling him skyward as the ground fell away beneath his boots. Gunner opened fire and tore chunks from the beast, and a thunderous shotgun blast finally shredded its hindquarters, forcing it to release Alabama. He hit the ground in a trained roll, battered and bleeding but alive, while the creature shrieked and fled into the dusk, trailing blood across the dirt.

In the aftermath, Elijah — one of the Marines accompanying the group — jammed a stim pack into Alabama's arm to stabilize him. Mackenzie, the party's scientist, crouched over the chunks of flesh the creature had left behind and found something deeply unsettling: the meat was still moving, still pulsing, slowly knitting itself back together. She noted the creature's mange-like symptoms and the unnatural regeneration, and the group quietly agreed that whatever was out here, it wasn't playing by normal rules. They pressed on toward the fissure.

The fissure itself was a massive, ragged wound in the planet's crust — a gash so wide and deep that its bottom was swallowed entirely by darkness. The edges crumbled at the slightest provocation, and a narrow, four-foot-wide switchback path wound its way down into the black. Lemmy Jet, the party's pilot and self-described expert, took one look at the path and decided the Warthog could make it. It could not. The vehicle's weight shattered the scree beneath its tires, the right side began to sink, and by the time Lemmy tried to correct course, the Warthog was already going over the edge. The rest of the party scrambled clear as the vehicle — and Lemmy with it — tumbled into the abyss below.

With their transport gone and their pilot lost, the survivors formed a single-file line and began the long, careful descent on foot. The temperature dropped steadily as they went deeper, and the dim light of Purgatory's eternal dusk faded to nothing. Then they saw the first one: a cloud of pitch-black gas hanging motionless in the air, radiating a cold so intense it was almost physical. Someone lobbed a rock through it as a test, and the rock came out the other side completely encased in ice before shattering against the wall. Mackenzie recognized the phenomenon — these frozen gas clouds were known to exist on the dark side of the planet, where they killed anyone who wandered into them — but she had never heard of them appearing in concentrated pockets like this, deep underground. The party wove carefully around the clouds and continued down.

After nearly an hour of slow, deliberate descent, the switchback path finally ended at a brushed metal door. The door's surface was covered in dazzling, fractal-like patterns that seemed to shift and multiply the longer anyone looked at them, filling the group with a creeping, irrational dread. Wiley jammed a crowbar into a panel beside the door, found a tangle of alien wiring beneath, and severed one of the cables. The door popped open with a hiss, releasing a cool breeze that carried with it the unmistakable stench of rotting meat. Gunner clicked on his hat light and stepped inside.

The room beyond was about thirty feet square, lined on both sides with shower stalls whose walls bore the same dazzling brushed metal as the door. Dried blood was caked into the stalls. A doorway at the far end opened onto a staircase that descended another twenty feet into a hallway, flanked on either side by alien computer terminals covered in symbols none of them could read. Gunner unclipped his dog — also named Gunner — from his leash and let him sniff ahead. The dog bounded confidently into a side room, and the party followed to find a laboratory divided into four partitioned sections, each containing a table scattered with sharp, blood-stained implements that looked disturbingly like antique dental tools. On the tables were body parts. On one of them, a mound of humanoid fingers with long, sharp nails sat piled like a grim trophy. Gunner the dog retrieved one and trotted it back to his owner, tail wagging.

Mackenzie took the finger to examine it and then dropped it. The moment it hit the floor, it began to move — rolling, bulging, sprouting antennae and then wings that beat the air with a high, thin hum. Kris Mauger stepped in and crushed it between his hands before it could do anything else. But the sound of its wings had barely faded before eleven more rose from the mound on the table and came screaming toward the group. They were fast, and they were purposeful, and what they wanted was to get inside someone's throat. Several of them succeeded. Kris, Gunner, and Wiley all found themselves choking as the creatures burrowed deeper, their sharp nails raking the insides of their esophagi. Alabama managed to swat his away, and Mackenzie went to work with her scalpel, slicing one out of Wiley's throat while the metallic blood it left behind made him gag. Wiley eventually burned his own attacker out with a butane lighter, scorching himself in the process, and shouted to the group that fire seemed to do the trick. Alabama bit down on the two lodged in his mouth, killing them — and swallowing a mouthful of their strange, metallic blood in the process.

The fight was brutal and chaotic, but eventually the last of the creatures was dead or removed. Gunner sealed his specimen in a dog poo bag and stomped it flat. Mackenzie attempted to stitch Wiley's tongue back together — it had split and bifurcated in four directions from the trauma — without anesthesia, while Wiley fought his own hand, which had begun moving entirely of its own accord. Then Kris felt a pop in his boot. He pulled it off to find his big toe splitting apart like torn paper, the split actively crawling up his foot in real time. Without hesitation, he grabbed a chainsaw and took his own leg off above the knee, then passed out cold on the laboratory floor. Elijah rushed in with a tourniquet and another stim pack, doing what he could to keep Kris from bleeding out, while an attempt to cauterize the stump with the butane lighter only made things worse.

The group made the only sensible decision left to them: they ran. With Kris unconscious and being carried, Wiley's tongue in ruins, and Alabama half-blind from the claw wounds he'd taken earlier, they began the long climb back up the switchback path. The frozen gas clouds still hung silently in the dark as they passed, indifferent. Then, somewhere near the top of the rise, Alabama's right eye simply exploded — a gout of blood pouring down his face as he staggered forward, now seeing the world through only one eye. He kept moving. They all kept moving.

They crested the top of the fissure and found something waiting for them. It stood eight feet tall, bipedal and humanoid, with three arms and skin covered in deep, ragged scars. Its eyes burned with a dull red glow that locked onto the battered survivors the moment they appeared. It reached across its body and drew a sword. The party stood at the edge of the abyss they had just climbed out of, bleeding and broken, with one bullet between them and a monster blocking the only way home.

A massive gash in the crust of Purgatory opened by a recent earthquake, with crumbling edges and a narrow, four-foot-wide switchback path winding down into pitch darkness. Pockets of deadly frozen gas hang in the air along the descent.
