# Episode 3: Cudassam

Date: 2026-06-18

## Summary
The perpetual dusk of Purgatory hung over Cudassam like a bruise that never healed, casting its amber half-light over the cafeteria where Ennis and Mike Jackson sat hunched over their morning gruel. Around them, the rough-and-tumble crews of loss prevention ate in silence, while students and faculty from the University of Idahl Minor huddled over their bowls at separate tables, and the teamsters of the Interstellar Crew Teamsters Guild sat caked in grime from a long night's work. Motivational slogans scrolled across screens mounted near the ceiling — "Productivity is Prosperity," "Your Sacrifice Matters" — though most people had long since stopped reading them. Then the loudspeaker crackled to life, and a voice identifying itself as Sinclair announced a surprise mandatory inspection by neighboring corporate offices, urging everyone to stay out of the inspectors' way and be on their best behavior.

The announcement stirred murmurs throughout the cafeteria. A man with thick-rimmed shaded glasses, dreadlocks, and a goatee leaned over and warned them that the last inspection, about a year prior, had not gone smoothly — eight people had disappeared without explanation, and the whole affair had simply petered out as fresh workers arrived to fill the gaps. Before he left, he also mentioned a secret religious gathering that evening: the Church of the Last Science, meeting at eighteen hundred hours on the fifth floor of the Beta building, in a quiet spot near the hanging gardens. He urged them to come if they were curious, and to make sure they weren't followed.

As the cafeteria began to empty, a malnourished man with slicked-back chestnut hair and sea-green eyes approached them with one arm hidden behind his back. He asked quietly if they had any leftover gruel, and both Ennis and Mike scraped what remained of their bowls into his. When he reached out to take it, he revealed his right hand — or what was left of it — several fingers had been blown clean off, leaving only stumps. He explained that he had gone out on a reconnaissance mission to an ancient ruin belonging to the Adai, the people who had inhabited Purgatory long before the company arrived, and had returned with no memory of what happened and missing fingers he couldn't account for. One of his squadmates, he had since learned, had been taken in for questioning and never seen again.

He introduced himself as Reginald and confessed that he was terrified of being found by corporate inspectors, convinced he was infected with something they wanted to study in a secret lab beneath the building. He begged for a place to hide, and after some reluctant negotiation, Ennis agreed to let him stay — on the couch, emphatically not under the bed. They arranged to meet at the bar later, and Reginald slipped away quickly, trying not to be seen. Before parting, he mentioned the names of others who had been on the mission with him: Winnetta, who worked in communications in the Alpha building, and Rick and James, both from loss prevention.

With time to spare before the evening meeting, Ennis and Mike decided to head to the sixth floor archives to look up what they could about Rick and James. The building's architecture was striking — each floor was laid out like a ship's wheel, with the spokes rotating slightly with each level, giving the whole structure a turntable quality as it rose. On the way to the archives, they passed a series of labeled doors: material science, fuel science, a bathroom — and one door with no label at all. Naturally, they opened it.

Inside was a brightly lit room trimmed in white and blue, with shelves of items arranged on display and terminals along one wall directing visitors to engage with them. Mike approached a terminal and found it was a corporate e-shop, offering various equipment for purchase. He bought a tear gas grenade, intending to use it alongside his infrared goggles in any future confrontation. But at the far end of the room was a black door, and both men felt an immediate, unnatural pull toward it — a compulsion that had them walking forward before they even realized it. They caught themselves, but the urge didn't go away.

Mike readied his rifle and had Ennis crack the black door open just enough to peer inside. The door slid upward to reveal a massive, pitch-black warehouse stretching fifty feet into the darkness, its shelves stacked with weapons, equipment, and supplies. Using his infrared goggles and headlamp, Mike swept the room while Ennis moved through the shelves, finding maintenance uniforms and a box of blank keycards. They gathered what they could, already forming a plan to use the uniforms as disguises and have the keycards programmed for access to the Alpha building. Then the arms dropped from the ceiling.

Three pairs of robotic limbs with horribly long fingers descended from above, accompanied by a glitching holographic torso wearing a shirt that read "Product Retention Team." The fight was brutal and disorienting — Mike's plasma rifle tore chunks of machinery from one of the units, exposing its internal circuitry, but the machines were relentless. Their fingers elongated into snake-like mechanical tendrils that coiled around both men, squeezing tight. Before either of them could break free, they were hoisted upward into the ceiling and swallowed by total darkness.

They hit an icy floor and immediately began to float. The temperature was sub-zero, the air barely breathable, and gravity had simply ceased to exist. Floating screens erupted around them, blasting infomercials and advertisements in every direction, while shelves of products encased in brown congealed muck drifted past — each one attached to a half-melted figure wearing what looked like a retail staff uniform. Ennis pulled his ushanka down over his ears against the cold. The experience felt like an eternity, and then, without warning, they were back in the hallway on the sixth floor. The unmarked door was gone. In its place was a blank wall, as though it had never existed. They still had the uniforms, the keycards, and the tear gas grenade.

Shaken but functional, they continued to the archives and searched the company directory. James was listed as killed in action, with no further details. Rick had been reassigned to the Domos mining station, about an hour away, and appeared to still be alive. Winnetta was listed as active — a short crew cut, a scar under one eye, and a past employee-of-the-month commendation from six months ago. With the information they needed, they headed down to the fourth floor cantina, which had just opened for the evening.

The bar was dim and smelled of cheap synthetic alcohol served in recycled glass mugs. The bartender, Amriel, listened with weary familiarity as they described what had happened to them, mentioning that rumors of interdimensional spaces occasionally opening up on Purgatory had been circulating for some time. Reginald arrived not long after, visibly anxious, and the party described the Product Retention Team to him. He was disturbed, and suggested that what they had found might represent an alternate way off the planet — though he wasn't sure it was a way anyone would want to take.

The journey back to Ennis's room was tense. At the elevator, the doors opened to reveal three corporate figures, the one in the center wearing a pink and black zoot suit and carrying himself with a cold, deliberate authority. He looked the group over slowly, questioned Ennis about his work and whether he had noticed any anomalies, and reminded them that strange behavior should always be reported. Reginald kept his ruined hand buried in his pocket and said nothing. The corporate trio exited on the fifth floor, and the party rode the rest of the way up in silence.

Ennis's room was tidy and orderly — he made sure of that, locking his extensive collection of adult magazines in a safe before Reginald could comment on them. Reginald was given the couch and seemed genuinely overwhelmed by the luxury of having that much space to himself. He explained his situation more fully: he believed he was infected with something that gave him flashes of pre-cognition, and that the corporation wanted to study him in a secret underground lab. His plan was to save enough supplies to attempt the pilgrimage — a seven-step spiritual journey said to be the only true way off Purgatory, with two of the seven beacons already active. No life could leave the planet otherwise, he said, except for the Gatekeepers, a corporate rank so high it was effectively unreachable. The session drew to a close with the party deciding to bring Reginald along to the Church of the Last Scion meeting that evening, planning to disguise his missing fingers with mittens so he wouldn't draw unwanted attention on the way.
